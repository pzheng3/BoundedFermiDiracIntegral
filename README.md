## Mathematical and computational changes

1. **Gamma series:** the general-order implementation uses the real Kummer inner moment, the correct outer gamma moment, and a clipped split for chemical potentials outside the interval. It is checked against quadrature and its finite-series remainder bound across 210 parameter combinations. The raw half-order series is not silently accelerated.
2. **Poisson evaluator:** the original singular-subtraction routine is retained in an isolated diagnostic namespace. The replacement uses a binomial mean of Fourier partial traces, with its scalar residual identity and a conservative exact-arithmetic truncation bound stated and tested in the notebook. N means the largest Fourier index, N=2L. This changes the numerical summation rule and must be reflected in the paper before these results are cited. The new evaluator does not use quadrature internally.
3. **Numerical inverse:** the corrected wrappers use explicit Brent tolerances and dynamically expand brackets outside the physical integration interval when necessary. Failures raise diagnostic exceptions rather than becoming silent NaNs. Original-integral residuals and chemical-potential errors are saved. The tested brackets succeed; global monotonicity of every finite approximation is not assumed.
4. **Analytic local inverse:** convergent logistic-polynomial derivative integrals feed explicitly defined ordinary Bell polynomials. Formal composition and independent target-value tests are performed without root finding.
5. **Sommerfeld inverse:** corrected order-dependent coefficients, the temperature-scaled tail, and the implicit asymptotic fixed-point iteration are tested separately. This is an interior low-temperature approximation, not a global inverse or a generally convergent infinite series.
6. **Hurwitz plots and inverse relations:** all seven requested orders appear together in forward and inverse-relation overlays on linear and logarithmic scales. The plotted function is explicitly the **normalized complete analytic continuation**, not the bounded unnormalized function used in the other plots. The order $c=-3/2$ has two real inverse branches, which are plotted separately and meet at the computed turning point.
7. **Precision audit:** an 80-digit evaluation of the exact binomial residual separates mathematical truncation from binary64 moment cancellation. It confirms continued convergence below $10^{-16}$ even though the double-precision implementation reaches a noisy floor.

The normalized Hurwitz curves satisfy the following definition.
$$\mathfrak F_c(\mu)=-\operatorname{Li}_{c+1}(-e^\mu).$$

For nonexceptional orders the evaluator uses the Hurwitz expression directly.
$$\mathfrak F_c(\mu)=\frac{2\pi(2\pi)^c}{\Gamma(c+1)\sin(\pi c)}\operatorname{Re}\left[e^{-i\pi c/2}\zeta\left(-c,\frac12-\frac{i\mu}{2\pi}\right)\right].$$

Derivative limits handle orders zero and one; the order-minus-one normalized limit is the logistic function.
$$\mathfrak F_{-1}(\mu)=\frac1{1+e^{-\mu}}.$$

At orders -1 and -3/2 the defining improper integrals diverge. At -1 the unnormalized continuation also has a pole, so a finite unnormalized seven-curve overlay would be misleading. The normalized convention is necessary to display all requested orders as finite curves. There are 35 checks against mpmath polylogarithms and independent integral/derivative formulas at 60 digits, plus a two-sided order-limit check at -1. These checks validate the plotted evaluations; they do not certify the manuscript's current contour proof.

A different summation method can improve endpoint behavior while being less accurate than the original subtraction at some interior points; the notebook plots both rather than claiming uniform superiority.

## Measured errors for the paper

Bounded tests use the unnormalized convention, c=1/2, a=10, T=1. The forward grid has 201 points in [0.1,7]. Inverse targets come from 82 chemical potentials in [0.5,9.5], including exactly 1. Reference checks use 60- and 80-digit quadrature at selected points; grid errors below compare with the double-precision quadrature baseline.

| N | Maximum raw forward error | Maximum new Poisson forward error | Maximum raw numerical inverse error | Maximum new Poisson numerical inverse error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 1.329447e-02 | 7.981118e-06 | 9.099547e-03 | 1.240100e-04 |
| 30 | 1.819129e-03 | 5.666578e-13 | 1.063487e-03 | 4.316547e-13 |
| 50 | 6.434834e-04 | 7.585044e-13 | 3.876228e-04 | 6.075140e-13 |
| 100 | 1.578357e-04 | 2.488676e-12 | 9.784238e-05 | 1.043610e-12 |

These are finite-grid observed maxima, not uniform analytic error bounds. Increasing the Poisson mode count beyond 30 does not monotonically improve the binary64 moment implementation because rounding, special-function error, and cancellation are already relevant. The separate 80-digit residual audit shows that the underlying binomial truncation continues to decrease below $10^{-16}$.

Actual quadrature and root settings are explicit.
$$\mathrm{epsabs}=\mathrm{epsrel}=10^{-12},\qquad\mathrm{xtol}=10^{-12},\qquad\mathrm{rtol}=4\epsilon_{\mathrm{machine}}=8.881784197001252\times10^{-16}.$$

These settings do not certify 1e-15 accuracy. Very small target values or near-saturation inverses require additional conditioning and precision analysis.

## Sources and manuscript consistency

corrected numerical tables and tolerances, the normalized negative-order
Hurwitz continuation, and separate numerical and analytical inverse tests. The
notebook, Python export, CSV files, and manuscript therefore use the same
definitions and numerical conventions.
